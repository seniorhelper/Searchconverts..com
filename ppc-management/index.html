The Highest Bid Does Not Win.

PPC advertising runs a live auction on every single search, and position is decided by bid multiplied by relevance. Advertisers who understand that pay less than their competitors for better placement. Everyone else just raises the bid. We are a PPC agency built around the half most accounts ignore.

How the auction actually works

Bid × relevance.
Move the second number and the first one matters less.

Search platforms do not sell position to the highest bidder. They rank advertisers on a score that combines the bid with how relevant and useful the ad is expected to be — Google calls its version Quality Score. A well-built account with a high score can outrank a competitor bidding significantly more, and pay less per click while doing it.

That is the entire arbitrage in paid search, and it is where a competent PPC agency or PPC company earns its fee. Drag the sliders below and watch what happens to your position and your actual cost per click.

The relationship shown here — that relevance discounts your cost and multiplies your bid — is the part that holds true everywhere and the part most accounts leave on the table.

Why this is the whole argument for hiring a PPC company

A PPC company that only knows how to raise bids has one lever, and it costs money every time they pull it. An advertiser who can lift relevance has a lever that reduces cost while improving position. That difference does not show up in a monthly report as anything dramatic. It shows up as a competitor quietly acquiring the same customers for 40% less, month after month, and eventually being able to outspend you on a smaller budget.

Relevance is built from tight keyword grouping, ad copy that matches the search rather than the brand, and a landing page that delivers what the ad promised. That last one is why we run conversion optimization alongside every paid account — the landing page is not downstream of the ad auction, it is an input to it.

Plain English

What is PPC management?

PPC management is the ongoing work of planning, building, running and improving pay-per-click advertising campaigns so that the money spent on clicks produces profitable customers. It covers keyword and audience research, account structure, ad creation, bidding strategy, negative keywords, landing page alignment, conversion tracking and continuous testing.

Pay per click advertising means exactly what it says: you pay only when someone clicks. That sounds low risk and is the reason so much money gets wasted on it. Nobody notices the cost of a click. They notice the invoice at the end of the month, by which point the account has bought several thousand of them and nobody can say which ones led to revenue.

A PPC agency exists to close that gap. Not to run ads — the platforms will happily run ads for you, and their automated recommendations are designed to increase your spend, not your profit. The job is to make every click accountable to a number that appears in your bank account.

Where the budget goes

Five ways a PPC account
quietly burns money

These are what we find in nearly every PPC advertising audit of an account that was set up once and left running. None of them look like a problem on the dashboard, which is exactly why they persist.

The leakWhat is happeningTypical cost
No negative keywords Broad match is showing your ads on searches containing the words "free", "jobs", "DIY", "salary" and your competitors' brand names. Every one of those clicks is billed. High
Tracking that lies The platform is counting page loads, thank-you page visits that fire twice, or form starts as conversions. It then optimizes hard toward producing more of them. Severe

Note what is missing from that list: bid amount. Almost nobody's core problem is that they are bidding too little. The money is lost before bidding is even the relevant question, and raising the budget on an account leaking in five places simply loses money faster.

Run your numbers

What can you actually
afford to pay per click?

This is the calculation that should precede every paid search decision and rarely does. Work backwards from what a customer is worth and the maximum viable cost per click falls out of it. If your current CPC is above that number, no amount of optimization saves the campaign — the economics do not work and something upstream has to change.

Cost per lead$163
Cost per sale$650
Profit per sale$550
Break-even CPC$12.00
Profitable, with room to bid. You break even at $12.00 a click and you are paying $6.50, so there is headroom to compete harder for better positions. The constraint here is volume, not economics.

Why this changes the strategy

Two businesses in the same industry can face completely different correct answers. One with a 2% conversion rate and a $400 profit per customer cannot afford $6 clicks at all and should fix the landing page before spending another dollar. One with an 8% conversion rate and a $3,000 profit can outbid nearly everyone and should be worried about volume, not cost.

Notice which input moves the break-even number fastest: the conversion rate. Doubling it doubles what you can afford to pay per click, which means you can buy positions your competitors cannot reach. This is why we treat conversion optimization and paid media as one engagement rather than two, and why an agency that only touches the ad account is working with one hand tied.

The question is usually the wrong one.

Agencies that sell only one of these will tell you theirs is better. We run both, so we have no reason to. The truthful answer is that they solve different problems on different timelines and the right split depends on how urgently you need customers and how long you plan to be in business.

How we work

What happens when you
hand us an account

  1. Audit before anything changes. We look at search terms, negative keyword coverage, tracking accuracy, account structure and landing page alignment. Roughly half the value we deliver in the first quarter is found in this step.
  2. Fix the tracking first. If conversions are being counted wrong, every decision after this point is wrong too, including the platform's own automated bidding. Nothing else starts until the numbers are trustworthy.
  3. Stop the bleeding. Negative keywords, pausing the searches that have never converted, and switching off auto-applied recommendations. This usually cuts waste before it improves anything.
  4. Rebuild structure. Tighter ad groups, ads written to the search rather than the brand, landing pages matched to the promise. This is where quality score and cost per click start moving.
  5. Then bid. Only once the account deserves cheap clicks do we start pushing for volume and position. Bidding aggressively into a badly built account is how budgets disappear.
  6. Test continuously. Ads, landing pages and offers, measured properly. Our approach to that is set out on the A/B testing page.
  7. Report on money. Cost per lead, cost per sale, return on ad spend. If a report cannot be connected to revenue, it does not go in the report.
