Plain definitions first

What is an AI agent, and how is it different from a chatbot?

A chatbot answers. An agent acts. A chatbot sits on your website and responds to questions. An agent is connected to your calendar, inbox, CRM or invoicing tool and does the next step: it texts the lead back, offers two open times, books the one they pick, adds the job to your system and reminds them the day before. It works from a written set of rules and hands anything outside those rules to a person.

Both are useful, and many businesses need only one. If what you really want is a well-trained assistant on your website that answers questions and captures contact details, a business chatbot may be all you need. If the work that eats your week happens after the question is answered (the follow-up, the scheduling, the chasing), that is agent work, and it is what this page is about.

Where the hours go

Which jobs should a small business hand to an AI agent first?

The best first agent takes a job that is frequent, rule-based and annoying, where a slow or forgotten response costs money. Here are the eight we are asked about most, with the guardrail we put on each.

Replying to new leads

Answers web forms, missed calls and texts within a minute, asks two qualifying questions and offers a time. Guardrail: never quotes a price that isn't on your approved list. For a sales-only build, see our AI sales agents.

Booking and rescheduling

Reads your real calendar, offers open slots, books, confirms and handles "can we move it to Thursday?" Guardrail: respects buffers, travel time and blackout days you set.

Quote and estimate follow-up

Checks in on open quotes on a schedule you choose, answers simple questions about the estimate, and flags anyone ready to sign. Guardrail: discounts go to a person, always.

Invoice reminders

Sends polite reminders before and after the due date with the payment link. Guardrail: stops on any reply that mentions a dispute and hands it to you.

Review requests

Asks happy customers for a review after the job is marked complete, with one follow-up. Guardrail: asks every customer the same way. No filtering who gets asked based on how they might rate you.

The same twenty questions

Hours, service area, what to expect, how to prepare. Answered from your own approved answers in email and text. Guardrail: says "let me get a person" when it isn't sure.

Data entry between apps

Moves a booked job from the inbox into the CRM, the CRM into the invoice, the invoice into the books. Guardrail: logs every change so you can see what it touched.

Daily summaries

A short morning message: new leads, today's jobs, quotes going cold, unpaid invoices. Guardrail: read-only. It reports, it doesn't act.

No-show recovery

Texts anyone who missed an appointment and offers a new time before the slot is lost for good. Guardrail: one attempt, then a person decides.

Where the line is

Which jobs should stay with people?

An agent is fast and tireless, and it is not a manager. We draw the line in the same places every time, and we write it into the rules before the agent goes live.

  • Pricing exceptions, discounts and refunds
  • An upset customer, a complaint or anything that sounds like a dispute
  • Legal, medical, tax or financial advice of any kind
  • Hiring, firing and anything about an employee
  • Signing contracts or committing your business to terms
  • Anything the agent has not seen before and cannot match to a rule

When an agent hits one of these, it does one thing: tells the customer a person will follow up, and puts the conversation in front of that person with the full history attached.

Safety and compliance

What rules keep an AI agent safe to run?

  • Least access. The agent gets only the permissions its job needs. A scheduling agent can read the calendar; it cannot see the bank account.
  • Approval points. You choose which actions need a thumbs-up before they happen.
  • A full log. Every message sent and every record changed, with time and reason, in one place you can read.
  • Consent for texting. Leads are texted only where they gave permission, with a working opt-out on every message.
  • An off switch. One setting pauses the agent and routes everything back to people.
  • Your data stays yours. Accounts are in your name, and you can walk away with every record.

How we build

How do we set up an AI agent for a small business?

One workflow at a time, proven before the next one starts. The goal is an agent you trust enough to stop checking every message, and the only way to get there is a short supervised period first.

Free 30-minute workflow audit

We walk through a normal week: where leads come from, what happens next, where things stall. You leave with the one job worth automating first, even if you never hire us.

Write the playbook

The exact steps a good employee would follow, your approved answers, your prices, your tone, and the list of things that must go to a person.

Connect the tools you already use

Calendar, inbox, phone and text, CRM, invoicing. No new software for your team to learn unless there is no other way.

Shadow mode

The agent drafts every message and action; a person approves or edits. Every edit teaches us something we write back into the playbook.

Go live with limits

Routine actions run on their own; exceptions still come to you. You see a daily log and can pause it at any time.

Monthly review

Response times, hours saved, hand-offs to people and anything it got wrong. Then we decide together whether the second workflow is worth building.

Two costs, kept separate: building the agent, and running it. How do you know an AI agent is working?

We agree on the measures before launch, and they are boring on purpose:

  • Time from a new lead arriving to the first reply
  • Share of leads that get a booked time
  • Hours your team no longer spends on the job, by their own count
  • How often the agent hands a conversation to a person, and why
  • Errors found in the log, and what changed in the playbook because of them

If those numbers do not move, the agent is not earning its keep, and we will tell you to switch it off rather than keep billing for it.
