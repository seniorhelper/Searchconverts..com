Choosing a conversion rate optimization company

Seven questions that separate CRO experts from people who redesign things.

Use them on us. Use them on everyone. The scorecard is right here.

Direct answer: To choose a conversion rate optimization company, ask seven things: their testing cadence and significance threshold; whether they asked what a customer is worth before quoting; whether they show live, clickable work; whether the copywriter has real sales experience; what they do when a test loses; whether they distinguish being found (SEO) from being chosen (CRO); and whether they were willing to tell you something you did not want to hear. The seven questions

What a real answer sounds like.

Below each question is the answer that should worry you and the answer you want. None of this is proprietary. The good agencies will be relieved you asked.

"How do you decide a test won?"

Worrying: "when the new version looks better." Good: a significance threshold, a minimum sample, and a rule for stopping early. Our A/B significance calculator is the math; ask to see theirs.

"What happens when a test loses?"

Worrying: silence, or "our tests don't lose." Good: "About half do. We learn why, revert, and the next test is smarter." Losing tests are the price of the winning ones.

"Who writes the words?"

Worrying: "our content team." Good: a named person who has sold something to a human being. The headline moves conversion more than any design change, and it's written by whoever understands the objection.

The other four, and the red flags

Live work, real numbers, and something you didn't want to hear.

"Can I click the work?" A deck of screenshots is not proof; a live site with a phone number in the first screen is. "What's a customer worth to me?" If they quoted before asking, they quoted a package, not a plan. "Is this a search problem or a conversion problem?" An agency that sells both as one thing will fix the one that's easier to bill. We're a conversion agency; the search side is our sister brand Eye To Ad Media, which publishes its own how-to-choose guide for a Denver SEO company.

And the seventh: did they tell you something you didn't want to hear? "Your site is fine, the follow-up isn't." "Don't buy ads yet." "That's not a job for us." An agency that never says no is selling, not advising. Red flags, plainly: guaranteed rates, references to tools that were discontinued years ago, month-to-month pricing with a twelve-month contract, and any pitch that starts with your logo.
