Plain English

What is an AI marketing agency?

An AI marketing agency, sometimes called an artificial intelligence marketing agency, does two distinct things, and it is worth separating them. The first is making your business visible inside AI systems — being named when someone asks an assistant for a recommendation. The second is using AI inside your marketing operation to produce work and handle tasks faster than a team could alone. Most firms calling themselves an AI digital marketing agency do only the second and describe it as the first.

We will be direct about something, because the category is full of noise: a great deal of what gets sold as AI marketing is ordinary marketing with a tool in the workflow. Using a language model to draft copy faster is useful. It is not a strategy, and it does not make a firm an AI marketing company or an AI advertising agency any more than owning a camera makes someone a film studio.

What has genuinely changed is where buyers now ask their questions. When somebody describes a problem to an assistant and gets three companies named back, the ranking that used to decide who they found has been replaced by a citation decision made inside a model. That decision runs on different signals than search rankings do, and the published research on those signals is where this page spends most of its time.

The uncomfortable part

Four of the five signals being scored are not on your website. They are mentions in other people's content, presence on third-party lists and reviews, video, and how often people search your name directly. That is a substantially harder problem than publishing a page, and it is why agencies prefer to sell you the page.

It also explains a pattern that confuses a lot of businesses: a site can rank respectably and be almost entirely absent from AI answers, while a competitor with worse rankings gets named constantly because they are talked about everywhere else.

Tool 02

One strategy cannot
cover both engines

Analysis of roughly 680 million citations found that only 11% of domains cited by ChatGPT are also cited by Perplexity. These are not two versions of the same thing. They retrieve differently, weight differently, and reward different work.

The gap in how often they name brands at all is even wider. A study of 34,234 AI responses reported ChatGPT citing brands 0.59% of the time, Perplexity 13.05%, and Grok around 27% — a spread of roughly 46 times.

The practical consequence is that work aimed at one engine frequently does nothing for another. Publishing a beautifully structured FAQ helps with live-retrieval engines and does very little for training-led ones, where the lever is being mentioned in places that get absorbed over months. Anyone promising a single tactic that wins everywhere has not looked at the overlap data.

The other half

What AI actually does well,
and what is theatre

Separate from visibility, there is the question every artificial intelligence marketing agency should answer plainly: where does using AI inside your marketing actually pay? Here is our honest split after building this on real accounts rather than in a pitch deck.

TaskVerdict
Instant lead responseGenuinely transformative. Research on response time is unambiguous, and no human team covers 9pm on a Saturday. This is the highest-return AI application in most businesses. See lead generation.
Creative volumeReal, with supervision. Producing enough concepts to test properly is a genuine constraint, and AI removes it. Reporting suggests AI-assisted creative can lift click-through meaningfully, largely through iteration speed. See Meta ads.
Qualification and routingWorks well. Asking the right follow-up questions and getting a lead to the right person is a bounded task with clear success criteria, which is where these systems are strongest.
Research and analysisUseful, verify everything. Excellent at surfacing and summarizing, unreliable on specifics. Every figure on this page was checked against a named source for exactly this reason.
Bid managementAlready happening. The platforms have automated this whether you asked or not. The skill is now supplying clean conversion signals rather than adjusting bids.
Unsupervised content publishingTheatre, and risky. Volume without editorial judgment produces pages nobody wants and, given how heavily AI systems weight quality signals, can actively cost you.
"AI-powered" dashboardsUsually theatre. A chart is a chart. If a tool cannot explain what decision it changed, the label is decoration.
StrategyNot yet. Deciding what to say, to whom, and whether the offer is any good remains judgment work. Anyone selling AI strategy is selling you a plausible-sounding average.

As an AI advertising agency and marketing company we build the first three for clients: instant response systems, qualification agents and creative pipelines. We are skeptical of the rest, and we would rather say so than sell you a dashboard.

Where we start,
and in what order

  1. Measure before advising. We query multiple assistants with the questions your buyers actually ask and record what comes back — for you and for competitors. Almost every engagement starts with a finding nobody expected.
  2. Check you have not opted out. Robots.txt, render-blocking and structured data. A surprising number of sites are invisible to AI systems for reasons that take an afternoon to fix.
  3. Read the platforms separately. Aggregate visibility scores hide everything that matters given roughly 11% overlap. Per-engine reporting or the number means nothing.
  4. Fix the instant-response layer. Before anything about visibility, make sure inquiries you already receive get answered immediately. It is faster, cheaper and compounds with everything downstream.
  5. Build mention surface, not just pages. Since brand mentions correlate far more strongly than backlinks, the work is being talked about in third-party content, lists, reviews, video and communities — not only publishing more of your own.
  6. Structure what you already have. Direct answers near the top of each section, consistent schema, clear headings. This is the part that helps live-retrieval engines most, and it runs through Eye To Ad Media.
  7. Deploy AI where it pays. Response, qualification and creative volume. Not dashboards.
  8. Re-measure monthly. This field moves faster than anything else in marketing. A tactic that worked in March may do nothing by September, and the only defense is measuring rather than believing.
