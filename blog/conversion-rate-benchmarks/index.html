Conversion Rate Benchmarks: What a Good Landing Page Actually Converts At

Direct answer: A typical landing page converts between 2% and 5% of visitors, with the median near 2.5% and the top decile above 10%; paid search and referral traffic convert higher than social, and mobile visitors convert more by phone than by form. The seven fixes that move a page from 2% toward 5% are headline match, phone placement, form length, proof, speed, a single call to action and counting calls as conversions.

Benchmarks are averages of pages you do not run. Here is how to read them, and the two numbers that matter more.

By Zachary Wennstedt · Updated September 21, 2026 · 9 minute read

Why benchmarks mislead more than they help

Every conversion benchmark you read is an average across thousands of pages you do not run, in industries you are not in, measuring outcomes you may not count. A dentist counting booked appointments and a store counting checkouts are not on the same scale. So use benchmarks for one thing only: to decide whether your number is so far off that something is broken.

The honest version: most landing pages convert between 2% and 6%; the median in Unbounce’s cross-industry benchmark reports has sat around 6% in recent years, with wide spread by industry. If you are under 2%, fix the page before buying more traffic. If you are over 10%, protect whatever is working and test around it.

What the published data says

Unbounce publishes a Conversion Benchmark Report drawn from tens of thousands of landing pages; WordStream publishes Google Ads conversion benchmarks by industry; and the form-length research most people cite also traces to Unbounce. The pattern across all three is consistent: legal, home services and health convert above the middle because the intent is urgent; ecommerce and B2B software convert below it because the decision is slower. Read the originals at Unbounce and WordStream; we summarize, we do not replace.

The two numbers that matter more than the benchmark

First: conversion rate by traffic source. Search traffic from someone typing your service converts several times better than a social click. If you blend them, a bad ad campaign can hide a great page. Second: conversion rate on mobile. WordStream’s recent data has the mobile share of conversions rising every year, and mobile is where most of your service customers call from.

For service businesses there is a third: calls. A page counting only form fills is under-reporting, because service customers call. If you count both, your real rate is often double the one in your analytics.

What moves a page from 2% to 5%

  • One job per page and one primary call to action
  • The headline states the outcome, not the company name
  • Fewer form fields; our form friction calculator prices each one
  • Proof where doubt shows up: reviews beside the price, photos beside the promise
  • A tappable phone number at the top and the bottom
  • What happens after they submit, written above the button
  • Page speed under three seconds on a phone

Every one of those is testable. Our A/B testing page explains what to test first and how much traffic it takes.

How to find your own benchmark

Pull ninety days of data. Split by source and device. Count calls. Now you have four or five real conversion rates instead of one blended one, and one of them is the leak. That number, not an industry average, is what you test against. If you want us to do the pull, the free strategy session is exactly that.

Questions readers ask

What is a good conversion rate for a landing page?
Most pages sit between 2% and 6%. Under 2% usually means a page problem; over 10% is excellent. Industry and traffic source change the range a lot.
Why is my conversion rate lower on mobile?
Slower load, harder forms, and buttons that are not thumb-reachable. Also, mobile service customers call rather than fill forms, so the form-only number undercounts them.
Do benchmarks apply to a small local business?
Only loosely. Your own rate by source and device is the number to improve. Use benchmarks to decide whether something is broken, not as a target.
How fast can a conversion rate improve?
Page fixes show within days once traffic arrives. A/B tests take two to four weeks each. Doubling a bad page is common; doubling a good one is rare.
