Two honest caveats, because this is the part of the industry where numbers get repeated without anyone checking them.

First, the widely quoted 87% figure gets attributed to Xerox, to CSO Insights, and to Gartner via the Association for Talent Development, depending on who is citing it. The underlying finding is consistent and the mechanism is well established, but treat the exact percentage as directional rather than precise.

Second, the curve above is a model, not a measurement of your team. It uses the published anchor points of roughly 70% loss at one week and 87% at one month, and applies the spacing effect that reinforcement produces. It is drawn to make a shape clear, not to predict your numbers to a decimal.

What is not in dispute is the direction. A single event decays fast. Spaced repetition, retrieval practice and coaching in the context of live deals flatten the curve, and the research on that is a century old and boring precisely because it keeps holding up.

The material

What actually gets taught

Not a methodology with a trademark on it. Four disciplines, chosen because they are where deals are usually lost, and taught against your real pipeline rather than a case study about a fictional company.

Consultative sales training

The core discipline, and the one most reps think they already do. Consultative selling means the diagnosis happens before the prescription, and that discovery is a real conversation rather than a checklist read aloud.

Most reps talk too much, ask questions with obvious answers, and present before they understand what the buyer is actually trying to fix. We work on question design, on genuinely listening to the answer, and on the discipline of not pitching for the first fifteen minutes. It is harder than it sounds and it changes close rates more than any script.

Sales negotiation training

Almost every deal that closes has a negotiation in it, and almost nobody trains for it. Reps discount because discounting ends the discomfort, not because the buyer required it.

We cover holding price without hostility, trading rather than conceding, spotting manufactured urgency, and the specific moment where a discount stops buying goodwill and starts signaling that the original number was invented. The most valuable habit is being able to sit in silence for four seconds.

B2B sales training for long cycles

When a purchase involves six to ten people and takes months, the skill is not persuasion, it is enabling a champion to win an argument you are not in the room for.

That means multi-threading beyond your one contact, building a business case someone can forward without editing, and understanding that most stalled deals are consensus failures rather than interest failures. The market context behind this sits on our B2B marketing page.

Sales leadership training

Whoever manages the team is the single biggest variable in whether any training survives. The research is unambiguous: reinforcement by managers is what flattens the curve.

So the leadership work is practical rather than inspirational. How to run a deal review that is not a status update, how to coach a call recording without crushing the person, what to inspect weekly, and how to hold a forecast conversation that produces truth instead of optimism.

AI sales training, which is mostly not what people expect

Every team now has access to tools that draft emails, summarize calls and score deals. The useful training is not a tour of the software. It is judgment about where a machine genuinely helps and where it quietly makes you worse.

Generated outreach is a good example. It is fast, it is grammatical, and buyers can smell it, which means volume goes up and reply rates go down. Call summaries are genuinely excellent. Automated coaching scores are useful for spotting patterns and terrible as a performance metric. Research is where the real gain sits, because a rep who arrives knowing the account's actual situation earns the small slice of buyer attention they get.

We train the boundary. What to automate, what never to automate, and how to use the transcript of your own calls as the most honest coaching material you will ever have. Where automation genuinely belongs in the wider funnel is covered on our AI marketing page.

One more area that fits here rather than in its own program: sales process and protocol. Written qualification criteria, a defined follow-up cadence, agreed stage definitions, and a handoff that does not lose people. This is closer to business consulting than to training, and for teams above a certain size it produces more than any skills work does, because a good rep inside a broken process still loses.

Buying advice

How to evaluate sales training companies

Including us. Most of this category sells confidence rather than evidence, so here are the questions that separate the two. Ask them of everyone you shortlist.

What happens after the session ends?

This is the whole question. If the answer is a workbook, a recording and an offer of further training, you are buying an event and the decay curve above tells you what happens next. Look for a defined reinforcement cadence written into the engagement, not offered as an upsell.

Who is in the room, and do they still sell?

Plenty of firms sell you a well-known name and deliver a certified associate working from a manual. Neither is automatically bad, but you should know which you are getting before you sign. Ask directly whether the person teaching has closed a deal recently, and in what market.

Is the material against your pipeline or a case study?

Generic role-play produces generic improvement. Training that works uses your actual deals, your real pricing, your genuine objections and, ideally, recordings of your own calls. If a provider has not asked to hear a call before quoting, they are selling a curriculum rather than solving your problem.

Are managers included?

For a team, this is close to decisive. Managers are the mechanism that keeps skills alive between sessions, so a program that trains reps and ignores leadership is buying a temporary improvement at full price.

What are they promising, and how carefully?

A specific guaranteed revenue lift, offered before anyone has seen your numbers or asked how long your deals take, is a sales tactic rather than a forecast. Good providers describe leading indicators, give ranges, and name their sources. Anyone quoting the famous statistics in this category without acknowledging that the attribution is contested has not checked them.

Will they tell you not to buy?

The most useful thing a provider can say is that training is not your constraint. If your leads are unqualified, your pricing is wrong, or the product does not do what the pitch claims, coaching will not fix it and a good advisor says so. We come at this from the marketing side as well, which is why we treat sales and demand as one system rather than two purchases. That perspective runs through everything we do as a conversion rate optimization agency.

Applied to us, honestly. We are a good fit if you want ongoing coaching against live deals from the person who owns the business. We are the wrong choice if you need a nationally recognized brand name on the invoice for internal reasons, a formal certification at the end, or a large-scale rollout across several hundred reps at once. Those are real requirements and other firms serve them better.

Questions I get

Corporate sales training questions, answered straight

Corporate sales training is structured development of selling skills for a company's team or its owner. It usually covers discovery and qualification, handling objections, negotiation, and managing longer multi-stakeholder deals. The important distinction is format rather than content: training delivered as a single event decays quickly, while training delivered as an initial session plus sustained reinforcement changes behavior. Research cited by the Association for Talent Development puts forgetting at 70% within a week and 87% within a month without follow-up, which is why we only work on a reinforcement model.

Because it is bought as an event and events decay. The Ebbinghaus forgetting curve, established in 1885 and replicated ever since, shows steep loss of new information within days unless it is deliberately revisited. Applied to sales, published figures put roughly 70% loss within a week and 87% within a month, and only around 13% of people apply new skills without ongoing reinforcement. The failure is rarely the content or the trainer. It is that the workshop ends, everyone returns to a full inbox, and nothing structured happens afterward.

Everything is quoted after a conversation, and we deliberately do not publish a rate. The reason is that an hourly number tells you almost nothing useful: coaching one owner through their own pipeline and running a reinforcement program for a twelve-person team are different engagements with different costs and different returns. Rather than anchor you to a number that may not fit, we would rather spend thirty minutes understanding whether training is even the right spend, then quote something specific. If it turns out you need marketing rather than coaching, we will say that too.

Consultative selling means diagnosing before prescribing. Rather than presenting a product and handling the resulting objections, the seller runs genuine discovery first and only proposes something once they understand what the buyer is trying to fix. Most reps believe they already do this. Recordings usually show otherwise: they talk more than they listen, ask questions whose answers they can predict, and present too early. Training it means working on question design, on actually listening to answers, and on tolerating the discomfort of not pitching immediately.

Holding price without becoming adversarial, trading concessions instead of giving them away, recognizing manufactured urgency and deadline pressure, and knowing the point at which a discount stops building goodwill and starts telling the buyer your original price was invented. Most discounting is not demanded by the buyer, it is offered by a seller who wants the discomfort to end. A surprising amount of the improvement comes from learning to stay quiet for a few seconds after stating a number.

It depends almost entirely on your sales cycle. If you close in a week, changes in behavior show up in the numbers within a month. If your cycle runs six months, you will see leading indicators first, such as more second meetings, better qualification, fewer deals dying silently, and less discounting, before revenue moves. Anyone promising a specific revenue lift on a specific date has not asked how long your deals take. The honest answer is that behavior changes within weeks and revenue follows at the speed of your pipeline.

The published research is encouraging and should be read carefully. CSO Insights reported that dynamic coaching processes improved win rates by 27.9% and quota attainment by 10.2%, and other studies find reps receiving continuous coaching exceed quota meaningfully more often than those getting sporadic training. Those are correlational findings from organizations that also tend to do other things well, so treat them as strong directional evidence rather than a guarantee. The calculator on this page applies that reported figure to your numbers so you can see the scale, not so you can bank it.
