What an audit actually checks

Twelve places a website talks people out of calling.

Almost every leak is one of the same dozen. The first screen doesn't say what you do. The phone number is under the fold. Five buttons compete for one click. The proof is on an "About" page nobody reads. The form asks for a fax number. None of them are hard to fix — but you can't fix the ones you can't see, and you can't see your own site like a stranger does.

The first screen

What a stranger sees before scrolling decides whether they scroll at all: the problem, the promise, the proof, the action. We check it on a phone, not a monitor.

The path to the call

How many taps from landing to talking to a human? Every extra one costs. We count them and cut them.

The trust stack

Reviews, photos, credentials, a real price signal — next to the offer, not hidden behind a menu. Machines and people both need to verify you.
