What a landing page has to do

It has one job. Most pages are doing nine.

A homepage introduces you. A landing page closes one specific visitor on one specific promise. The moment it also tries to show your history, your team, your seven services and your blog, it stops closing. The best landing pages are almost boring in their focus — and they convert two to five times better than the homepage they replaced.

Message match

The page says what the ad said, in the same words. A visitor who clicked "same-day water heater repair" should not land on "plumbing services."

Proof beside the ask

Reviews, photos, a credential, a price signal — within one screen of the button. Trust is built next to the decision, not on another page.

Speed and one action

Under three seconds on a phone. One button, repeated. A form that asks for a name and a phone number and nothing else it doesn't need.

How we build and test

Build it right. Then let the data argue.

We start with the ad or channel — who clicks, what they were promised, what they need to believe before they act. The page is written around that: headline, sub-headline, offer, proof, action, in that order. Speed is engineered in, not optimized later. The form is the shortest one that still lets you call them back.

Then we test. Headline variants, offer variants, form length — one change at a time, run to significance with the same math our A/B significance calculator uses. Winners stay. Losers teach. The page keeps getting better while the ads keep running.
