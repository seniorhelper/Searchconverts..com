Anatomy of a conversion · interactive

What actually happens between a search and a phone call.

Six steps. Break any one and the customer is gone.

This page walks through a real conversion the way a stranger experiences it — and shows you, on the phone screen, what each step looks like when it's wrong and when it's right. Tap the steps. Then flip the switch and watch the same page go from locked to open.

Walk through itAudit my site
The switch

Same page. Two versions.

Flip it and watch six things change on the phone below: headline, phone, proof, button, form length, response.

Locked: the headline names the company, the phone is hidden, proof is faded, two buttons compete, the form asks for twelve things, and nobody replies.

Direct answer: a website conversion has six steps — the search, the first screen, the offer, the proof, the action and the follow-up — and each one is a place a prospective customer can leave. Search Converts optimizes the last five (conversion) and coordinates the first (search visibility) with Eye To Ad Media. The interactive phone on this page demonstrates each step in a failing and a working state.

1The search

A stranger types the thing you do into Google — or asks their phone, or ChatGPT. If you're not in the answer, nothing below happens. That's the search half: Eye To Ad Media.

2The first screen

They land. In about five seconds they decide: do these people do what I need, for people like me, and what do I do next? The headline names their problem. The phone number is where a thumb reaches it.

3The offer

What happens next, and what does it cost to find out? "Free inspection." "Get a price in one call." A clear next step with the risk removed. Not "learn more."

4The proof

Reviews, photos, a credential, a real price signal — beside the button, not on another page. Trust is built at the moment of decision.

5The action

One button. One color. The same on every screen. A form with five fields, not twelve. On a phone, "call" is the action; everything else is a detour.

6The follow-up

They called or sent the form. The first hour decides whether they become a customer. Speed, then sales: tie-downs, trial closes, price conditioning. A lead nobody answers is a locked door with the lights on.
