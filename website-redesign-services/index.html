Before anyone opens a design file

When does a website actually need a redesign?

Less often than most agencies will tell you. A redesign is the right call when the foundation is the problem: the platform is no longer supported, your team cannot change a sentence without breaking a layout, the site is slow on phones no matter what gets compressed, or the business has changed enough that the old structure no longer describes what you sell. If the foundation is fine and the site simply does not produce calls, the cheaper answer is usually to fix the pages, not replace them.

The expensive mistakes

What goes wrong in most website redesigns?

Most failed redesigns look great on launch day. The damage shows up three to eight weeks later, when calls slow down and nobody can say why. These are the causes we see over and over when a business brings us a site that someone else rebuilt.

Pages that ranked were deleted

A service page written years ago quietly brought in a third of the leads. The new sitemap "simplified" it away, and there was no redirect. The traffic did not move to the new page. It moved to a competitor.

URLs changed without a map

Every old address needs a one-to-one 301 redirect to its closest new match, not a blanket redirect to the homepage. Google's guidance on site moves is to keep those redirects in place for at least a year.

Design came first, words last

When the copy is poured into a finished layout, the headline gets cut to fit a box. The page looks right and says nothing. We write the first screen before we design it.

Nobody recorded a baseline

If you don't know how many calls and forms the old site produced per month, you can't prove the new one is better. Or catch it early when it isn't.

Forms and call tracking broke

A form that sends to an old inbox, a tracking number that disappeared from the header, a thank-you page that no longer fires the conversion. Small wiring, big losses.

Heavy themes replaced fast pages

Sliders, autoplay video and a dozen plugins make a beautiful demo and a slow phone experience. Speed is a design decision, made before the build, not a fix after it.

If search rankings are the bigger worry, it is worth getting a free SEO audit before the rebuild so the pages that bring in organic traffic are flagged before anything is touched.

How we work

How does our website redesign process work?

Every redesign we run follows the same six stages. The order matters more than the tools: inventory before design, words before layout, tracking before launch.

Inventory and baseline

We crawl every URL, pull the pages that earn clicks and links, and record what the current site produces each month in calls, forms and bookings. That number is the bar the new site has to clear.

Keep, merge or retire

Each URL gets a decision. Pages that rank stay at the same address where possible. Thin pages are merged into stronger ones. Anything retired gets a 301 to its closest match, written into a redirect map you can open in a spreadsheet.

Write the money pages first

Homepage, top service pages, contact. A headline that names the problem, one line under it, a call button and a short form in the first screen, proof beside the offer. Conversion copywriting is part of the job, not an add-on.

Design and build

On the platform that fits how you work: hand-coded for speed, or WordPress, Shopify or Webflow when your team needs to edit daily. Built mobile-first and checked on real phones.

Pre-launch QA

Every redirect tested, every form submitted, every phone link tapped, schema validated, speed measured on a mid-range phone. Nothing launches on a Friday.

Launch and a 90-day watch

We watch Search Console for crawl errors and 404s, compare leads against the baseline week by week, and fix what slipped. Then we start testing the pages that matter most.

What you get at the end

  • A URL inventory and redirect map you own
  • Rewritten copy for every money page
  • A mobile-first design system, not a one-off theme
  • Call, form and booking tracking tied to the source
  • Structured data so search engines and AI assistants can read the business clearly
  • A before-and-after lead report at 30, 60 and 90 days
  • Logins to every account. You own the site, the domain and the data

Planning

How long does a website redesign take?

Long enough to do the inventory properly, and no longer. A small service site moves much faster than a large catalog, and the biggest variable is usually content: how quickly you can answer questions about your offer, approve copy and send photos of real work. You get a dated schedule with the quote, with the launch date tied to the approvals it depends on.

The part people forget is after launch. Search engines need time to recrawl moved pages, and some ranking movement in the first weeks is normal when URLs change. That is why the watch period is built into every project instead of sold as an extra.

What do you need from us to start?

  • Access to the domain registrar and current hosting (or a person who has it)
  • Google Search Console and analytics access, so we can see which pages earn traffic today
  • Where calls and form leads go now, and how you count a good lead
  • Thirty minutes to tell us what sells, who buys and what customers ask before they hire you
  • Real photos of your work, team or product if you have them; stock photos are the last resort
