That is conversion rate optimization in one click.

Almost everyone picks Version B, and the reasons are not aesthetic. Four separate, well-documented conversion principles are working at once:

  • Outcome over action. "Submit" describes what the visitor gives up. "Get my free plan" describes what they receive. Button copy that names the reward consistently outperforms copy that names the mechanic.
  • Contrast directs attention. The eye goes to the element with the highest contrast against its surroundings. If your primary action does not visually dominate, visitors have to hunt for it — and a meaningful share simply will not.
  • Target size lowers effort. Fitts's law: time to acquire a target falls as the target grows. On touch screens this stops being a nicety and starts being the difference between a tap and a mis-tap.
  • First person beats second person. "My plan" reads as the visitor's own internal voice. "Your plan" reads as the company talking at them.

Teardown

Anatomy of a page
that actually converts

Six elements do most of the work. Miss one and the page still looks fine — it just quietly earns less. Here is what we look for first in every audit.

1
A headline that names the outcome

Visitors decide in a few seconds whether they are in the right place. Say what they get, not what you are.

2
One dominant call to action

Two equally weighted buttons is a decision. A decision is friction. Pick the primary action and make it obvious.

3
Trust signals above the fold

Reviews, guarantees, credentials and years in business answer the objection before it forms.

4
The shortest form that still qualifies

Every extra field costs completions. Ask for what you need to make the call, and nothing else.

5
Proof, not adjectives

Specific results, real names and real numbers outperform "industry-leading" every single time.

6
Speed as a conversion feature

Load time is not an engineering metric. It is the first thing every visitor experiences, and slow pages lose people before a word is read.

Plain English

What is conversion rate optimization?

Conversion rate optimization (CRO) is the practice of increasing the percentage of website visitors who take a desired action — submitting a form, calling, booking, or buying. Instead of paying for more traffic, conversion optimization increases the value of the traffic a business already receives, using research, testing and measured changes to the page, offer and user experience.

Your conversion rate is simply conversions divided by visitors. A site with 10,000 monthly visitors and 200 leads converts at 2%. Raise that to 3% and you get 300 leads from the identical traffic — a 50% increase in output with no increase in spend. That is the whole idea, and it is why conversion optimization usually returns more than adding budget to the same broken funnel.

What a conversion rate optimization agency actually does

  • Research first. Analytics review, heat mapping, session recordings, form analytics and customer interviews to find where people are leaving and why.
  • Prioritize by impact. Not every problem is worth fixing. The highest-traffic, highest-drop-off steps get attention first.
  • Build and test. New pages, copy, forms and flows, tested against the original rather than swapped on a hunch.
  • Measure honestly. A test that loses is information, not a failure. A test with too little traffic to be conclusive gets called that way.
  • Fix the plumbing. Page speed, mobile behavior, broken tracking and form errors quietly cost more conversions than most design decisions.

What is a good conversion rate?

It depends heavily on industry, traffic source and what counts as a conversion. Lead-generation sites commonly sit between 1% and 3%; ecommerce often runs lower; a well-targeted paid landing page can run far higher. The number that matters is not the benchmark — it is whether your rate is improving against your own baseline, and whether the resulting customers are profitable.

CRO, SEO and paid media are not competitors

Search optimization and advertising decide how many people arrive. Conversion optimization decides what share of them become customers. Improving the conversion rate raises the return on every other channel simultaneously, which is why we start there and why it makes the paid media that follows cheaper per acquisition. It is also why performance marketing agencies that ignore the landing page tend to plateau: at some point you cannot buy your way past a page that does not convert.
