Agent-ready conversion: how AI agents complete, or abandon, your funnel

Direct answer: Agent-ready conversion is the practice of making a website convertible by software acting for a customer: an AI assistant that researches, compares, books or buys on a person's behalf. Agents complete a funnel when the offer, price, availability and next step are stated in machine-readable form and the form or booking endpoint is simple; they abandon it when the facts live in images, chat widgets or multi-step forms. The fix is a short checklist, not a rebuild.

Conversion optimization has always been about removing doubt and friction for a stranger. The newest stranger is not a person. It is a model with a budget and a task, and it leaves faster than any human ever did.

Who the new visitor is

Three kinds of agent now land on business websites. Answer engines (AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, Perplexity) read your pages to answer a question and decide whether to cite you. Research agents compare several providers against a brief, build a shortlist and hand it back to a person. Action agents go further: they fill the form, book the slot or complete the checkout, with the user's consent, and report back. The first group decides whether you are seen; the second whether you are shortlisted; the third whether the enquiry or the order actually arrives.

Each one is a conversion event you can win or lose, and each is lost for reasons that look nothing like the reasons humans leave. A human forgives a phone number rendered as an image; an agent cannot dial it. A human will fight through a six-step quote wizard if the price is right; an agent abandons at step two because it cannot see where the steps end.

Where agents abandon

Facts trapped in pixels

Prices in a JPEG price list, the phone number as an image, hours in a screenshot of a sign. A person reads them; a crawler and most agents do not. Every fact that decides a purchase needs to exist as text or structured data.

Offers that depend on a conversation

"Call for pricing" is a human conversion path. To an agent comparing five providers it reads as "unknown", and unknown loses to a stated range every time. State a floor, a range or a typical figure.

Forms that cannot be understood

Fields without labels, custom dropdowns built from divs, CAPTCHAs on first contact, and multi-step wizards with no indication of length. Agents fill what they can parse; everything else is friction they route around by choosing someone else.

Chat widgets as the only path

If the only way to ask a question or book is a proprietary chat bubble loaded by script, an agent has nothing to act on. Keep a plain form, a tel: link and a mailto or booking URL in the HTML.

Availability that is not stated

Booking agents need to know whether you can take the job this week. "Next available: Thursday" as text, or a booking endpoint with real slots, converts; "we'll get back to you" does not.

Inconsistent entity facts

Three phone formats, two business names and a different address in the footer and the schema. Agents cross-check; inconsistency lowers confidence and confidence decides the shortlist.

The agent-ready checklist

These are the items we check on client sites, in the order they usually matter. Most take an afternoon.

  1. Every decision fact as text. Price or range, service area, hours, response time, what is included, what is not. If it is on a slide or an image, write it down in HTML too.
  2. One Organization node, consistent everywhere. Name, phone, email and area served identical in JSON-LD, footer and contact page. One phone format. Search Converts publishes one @id and one number for exactly this reason.
  3. A stated offer on every money page. What you do, for whom, at what price point, and what happens after they contact you, written as a short "Direct answer" paragraph near the top.
  4. A simple, labelled form. Native inputs with label for, name attributes that mean something (phone, not field_7), four to five fields, no CAPTCHA before the first attempt, and a plain success state.
  5. Actionable contact links. tel: and mailto: or a booking URL present in the HTML, not injected only after a click.
  6. Availability or response time stated. "A person replies within one business day" is enough for a service business; a store needs delivery dates; a clinic needs next available appointment.
  7. Machine-readable summaries. An llms.txt, and where it is worth it, a small JSON file describing services, pricing and contact. Ours are documented at /api/.
  8. Schema actions where they are true. potentialAction with a CommunicateAction for calling, a ReserveAction for a real booking endpoint, an OrderAction for a checkout. Never declare an action the site cannot complete.
  9. No fake gates. Agents report what they find. A "free guide" that requires a phone number and triggers a sales call is reported as a gate, and the user is steered elsewhere.
  10. Measure agent traffic separately. Known agent user agents and referrers in a separate analytics segment, so you can see whether they convert and where they stop.

A schema recipe for a bookable service

This is the shape we use for a business that takes bookings. It declares the organisation once, states the service and price range, and tells an agent that a reservation is possible and where. Replace the values; do not declare a ReserveAction unless the target really books.

The visible page should say the same thing in words: the $89 diagnostic, the booking link, and when the next slot is. Agents that find the schema and the copy agreeing treat both as reliable.

What this does to human conversion

Every item on the checklist also helps a person. Stated prices reduce bounce from price-sensitive visitors who would have left anyway and raise the quality of the leads who stay. Labelled, short forms complete more often. A consistent phone number is tapped more often. In our own conversion work the overlap is almost total, which is why we treat agent readiness as part of conversion rate optimization rather than a separate project. The one genuinely new habit is writing the direct answer: a two or three sentence paragraph under the headline that states what you do, for whom, and on what terms. Humans skim it; agents quote it.

Where it matters most is the middle of the funnel. An agent that shortlists you and then hands the user a phone number has done your lead generation; whether that becomes a customer depends on how fast a human picks up and whether the call is counted at all.

Questions people ask

What is agent-ready conversion?

Making a website convertible by AI agents acting for a customer: stating price, availability and the next step as text and structured data, keeping forms simple and labelled, and exposing tel, mailto or booking links in the HTML so an agent can complete the enquiry or purchase.

Do AI agents really complete forms and bookings?

Action agents from the major assistant platforms can fill forms, book slots and complete checkouts with user consent, and their share of traffic is growing from a small base. Research and answer agents, which read and compare, already touch most business websites.

Will stating prices lose me leads?

It usually loses enquiries that would not have bought and raises the share that do. For agents it is decisive: an unknown price is dropped from a comparison.

Does this replace conversion optimization for humans?

No. It is a subset of it. Every checklist item also helps people; the only new work is writing the direct answer and publishing the machine-readable summaries.

How do I know whether agents visit my site?

Segment known AI user agents and referrers in analytics and watch server logs. Most sites see answer-engine crawlers daily and action agents occasionally.

What should I do first?

Get every decision fact into text, fix the Organization schema so it is consistent, and shorten the form. Then add the direct answer paragraph to your money pages.
